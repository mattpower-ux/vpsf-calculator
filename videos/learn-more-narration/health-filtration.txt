Indoor air can carry dust, pollen, smoke particles, and pollutants released by everyday products. Better air quality starts by reducing sources, then moving and cleaning air deliberately.

Filtration captures particles as air passes through the heating and cooling system or a dedicated cleaner. A more effective filter only helps if the fan and duct system can move enough air through it.

Moisture deserves equal attention. Bathroom and kitchen exhaust, leak repairs, and humidity control help prevent damp surfaces and the problems that follow.

Choose low-emitting finishes when replacing materials, and avoid storing chemicals near living spaces or air intakes. Sensors can reveal trends, but they do not replace inspection or maintenance.

Keep filter specifications, ventilation details, humidity controls, and service records. Together, they show how the home manages indoor air in practice.
